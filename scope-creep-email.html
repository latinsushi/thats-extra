How to charge a client for extra work

Do it the same day. Waiting makes the extra feel like it was always included.

  1. Quote the ask in their words. “Can you also…” is the evidence.
  2. Restate the original scope in one breath. Not a lecture. A line.
  3. Name a number. Hourly or flat. Silence is a discount.
  4. Say when work starts: after written approval. A reply counts.

Why this feels rude (and why it is not)

Independent designers, developers, and consultants get paid for a defined job. A new page, a new round, or a new integration is a new job. Naming the price is how you stay a shop, not a favor.

You do not need a lawyer. You need a clear email and, if you want a signature, a one-page change request.

What not to write

  • “Happy to squeeze this in.” That trains them to keep squeezing.
  • “This might cost more.” Might is how you look cheap.
  • “Sorry to even ask.” You are not asking for a favor. You are pricing work.
